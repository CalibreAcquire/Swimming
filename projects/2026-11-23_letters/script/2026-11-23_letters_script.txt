North London replied at 1.4%, nearly five times higher.

You'd expect North to sell more. It didn't.
East London sold nine buildings.

Off-market gets misunderstood, so it's worth being clear about it.

It doesn't mean cheap.
We don't sell below market value properties.
Every building we sell is valued on its income, its condition and its location,
the same as it would be on a portal.

It isn't a secret club either.
It's knocking on the door before the "for sale" sign goes up.

What it gives a buyer is time.
The investors on our list see a building
before it's advertised anywhere public.
If it isn't under offer by then, it goes to a full public launch,
and everyone else sees it after they've had first look.

The buyers who get these buildings are rarely the ones with the most money.
They're the ones who are ready.
There are four things to have in place.

First, your criteria.
Write down the areas, the type of building,
your price range and the minimum yield you'd accept.
If you can't say it in one sentence,
an agent can't match you to anything.

Then there's finance.
Have an agreement in principle or proof of funds before you view,
not after you offer.

There's also your solicitor.
Line up one who does commercial and mixed-use work
before you find the building.
A residential conveyancer can slow a commercial deal down by weeks.

And then there's speed.
When the right building comes up,
view it within days and decide within a week.
Good buildings don't wait for second viewings.
