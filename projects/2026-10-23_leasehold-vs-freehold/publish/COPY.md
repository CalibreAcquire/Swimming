### 1st: Reel 1 (What you actually own), with the YouTube video
```
Freehold vs leasehold: what you actually own.

- Freehold means you own the building and the land it sits on outright. No landlord above you, no lease to run down and no ground rent.
- Leasehold means you own the right to use the property for a fixed number of years set out in the lease. The freehold is owned by someone else.
- With leasehold you'll typically pay ground rent and, depending on the lease, a service charge, and you may need the freeholder's permission for certain changes.
```

### 3rd: Reel 3 (The repairs are yours)
```
Buying freehold? The repairs are yours.

- As the freeholder you're responsible for all external works: the roof, the structure, the fabric of the building.
- There's no freeholder above you to share those costs with.
- A significant repair bill lands entirely with you, and that needs to be factored into how you hold and manage the asset.
```

### 4th: Reel 4 (Freehold isn't automatically better)
```
Freehold isn't automatically the better investment.

- A leasehold flat with 900 years remaining and a low ground rent behaves very differently to one with 60 years left and an escalating rent clause.
- I wouldn't rule out leasehold completely, but I'd want a strong gross yield going in, so the service charge and ground rent don't leave me with a low net yield.
- What matters is the specific lease terms or freehold title, not the label on the listing.
```
