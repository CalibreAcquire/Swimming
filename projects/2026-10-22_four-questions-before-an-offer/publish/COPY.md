### 1st: Reel 1 (Who's paying the rent?), with the YouTube video
```
Before you offer, ask: who's paying the rent, and how secure is it?

- If the property is tenanted, you're not just buying a building, you're buying the tenant's ability to keep paying.
- Ask how long they've been in occupation, whether they've ever missed a payment, and how long is left on the lease.
- The asking price can look the same and reflect the same yield, but it can cause issues further down the line.

### 2nd: Reel 2 (What does it actually cost?)
```
Before you offer, ask: what does this actually cost beyond the purchase price?

- Service charge, ground rent, repairs and planning constraints don't show up in the headline figure.
- Ask for the last three years' service charge accounts if there's one in place.
- Ask whether any major works are planned or already agreed.
- A property that looks cheaper on paper can end up costing a lot more.

### 3rd: Reel 3 (Why is the vendor selling?)
```
Before you offer, ask: why is the vendor actually selling?

- It shapes how the negotiation goes and what you might uncover.
- A vendor selling because of probate, retirement or a change in circumstances behaves differently to one selling because the property has become a problem.
- Ask your agent or solicitor what they know. The reason behind a sale often explains things the listing won't.

### 4th: Reel 4 (Who would buy it later?)
```
Before you offer, ask: who would buy this from me later?

- A property with broad appeal, a good location, flexible use and no unusual lease structure is easier to sell on.
- A highly specific asset can be harder to move, even at a fair price.
- You don't need to plan an exit on day one, but if you can't picture who'd want it in five or ten years, that's worth considering before you offer.
